## What these are for

Decks are published in **BATIS**, the official Turība system. They are not in
this repository. The pre-reading tables name a deck and a slide range.

Each file states, before the session:

- **what you will be able to do** by the end of it, as testable statements
- **what to read beforehand** — specific slides and sections, not "the deck"
- **the hard parts** — where people actually get stuck
- **self-check questions** you use to find your own gaps before the session
- **what the assignment will require** of you

This is a contract in both directions. Nothing in the session, the deck or the assignment will require anything that is not on that page. In return, the session assumes you have read it.

## Attendance, and what a session is for

You are expected to attend, and attendance is registered by scanning a QR code
in the session. **Staying is your own decision** — see
[SYLLABUS.md](../SYLLABUS.md) §11, which is provisional pending confirmation
against university regulation.

**No content depends on being in the room.** Everything you need to pass this
course is published here — the objectives ahead of each session, the decks as
pre-reading, the assignment brief, the public tests. That is deliberate, and it
is the whole reason these files exist seven days early.

Treat a session as what it actually is: **195 minutes of direct access to
someone who has built and operated these systems for a living.** Not a
delivery mechanism for material you could read.

Which means the reason to stay, once you have scanned, is entirely what you
bring to it:

| | |
|---|---|
| 20 min | Overview — the outcomes and the hard parts |
| 40 min | **Q&A — your questions.** Written down, before you arrive |
| 15 min | Break |
| 90 min | **Open lab** — you work, I circulate |
| 30 min | Assignment briefing and wrap |

The decks are **pre-reading, not delivery**. Use the self-check questions to
find out which of the hard parts you cannot yet explain, and bring those. A
Q&A where nobody has prepared anything is forty minutes of me talking, which
is exactly the thing you could have read instead.

## How these pages are written

These pages use **ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English**. That is a
controlled-language specification from the aerospace industry. It exists so
that a reader with English as a second language reads a technical document one
time and understands it.

What it means when you read these pages:

| Rule | Effect |
|---|---|
| One idea in one sentence | Sentences are short. No sentence has more than 25 words |
| Active voice | "You must commit the specification", and not "the specification must be committed" |
| One word, one meaning | The same term always means the same thing. I do not use a synonym for variety |
| No idiom, and no metaphor | The text says what it means |
| Simple present tense | "The broker keeps the message", and not "the broker will have kept the message" |

The pages read in a flat style. That is deliberate. The decks and the session
keep a normal voice; these pages are a contract, and a contract must be
unambiguous.

### The objective verbs

Each objective starts with one verb from this list. The verb tells you what
level of work the assignment and the exam expect from you.

| Verb | What you must be able to do |
|---|---|
| **Explain** | Give the meaning in your own words, to another person |
| **Identify** | Find the item, and name it correctly |
| **Recognise** | See the pattern in a new example that I did not show you |
| **Read** | Take the correct meaning out of a diagram or code that you did not write |
| **Apply** | Use the method to get a correct result |
| **Write** | Produce the artefact yourself |
| **Model** | Draw the process with correct notation |
| **Compare** | Give the difference, and one condition where each option is correct |
| **Select** | Choose one option, and give a reason for the choice |
| **Analyse** | Take the thing apart, and name the property that causes the behaviour |
| **Evaluate** | Judge the option against a stated requirement |
| **Design** | Produce a solution, and defend the trade that you accepted |
| **Define** | State the boundary of a term or a measurement |
| **Relate** | Connect a technical choice to a business constraint |

These verbs are technical terms in this course. They are the reason a mark is
what it is. An objective that says **Explain** does not ask you to implement.
An objective that says **Design** does not accept a description.
