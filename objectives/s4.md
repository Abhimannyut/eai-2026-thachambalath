## By the end of this session, you can

1. **Identify** the transformation patterns: message translator, content enricher, content filter and claim check.
2. **Explain** the N times M problem. Calculate the number of translators with a canonical model and without one.
3. **Apply** a canonical model. Map three source formats to one shape.
4. **Apply** a content enricher. Get the missing data from a service.
5. **Apply** a content filter. Remove the data that must not go forward.
6. **Explain** schema evolution. Identify a change that stops a consumer.
7. **Apply** the tolerant reader pattern.
8. **Explain** why a money amount must be a decimal string, and not a floating-point number.

## The hard parts

These are the parts that people find difficult. Read them before the
session. If you cannot follow one of them, make it your question.

- **The canonical model is not the sum of the source formats.** It is a separate model. It has one owner, and the source systems do not control it.
- **A canonical model has a cost.** It replaces one translation with two: source to canonical, then canonical to target. You accept that cost to remove the N times M growth.
- **A schema change stops a consumer without an error message.** A field disappears, and the consumer reads an empty value. The tolerant reader pattern is the defence.

## Self-check

Answer these before the session. They are not graded, and I do not
collect them. They show you which of the hard parts you cannot yet
explain. **A question that you cannot answer is your question for the
session.**

1. Five source systems must send data to four target systems. How many translators do you need with a canonical model, and how many without one?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Without a canonical model you need up to 20 translators, which is 5 times 4. With a canonical model you need 9, which is 5 plus 4. The saving increases with each new system.

   </details>

2. A source system adds a new field to its message. Your consumer does not know the field. What must your consumer do?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   It must ignore the field and continue. This is the tolerant reader pattern. A consumer that fails on an unknown field makes every producer change a coordinated release.

   </details>

3. Why is the amount 45,20 not safe as a floating-point number?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   The value becomes 45.2. The trailing zero disappears, and the output no longer matches the source. Money needs a decimal string, or an integer number of cents.

   </details>

## What the assignment will require

**PA4 — three sources to one canonical model**

You map three input formats to the canonical order model. You then enrich the result from a pricing service.
The canonical model in `canonical/order.schema.json` is mine. You conform to it. You do not change it.
The assignment assumes that you can do these things:
- Read nested JSON, flat JSON and XML.
- Map a field name and a field value to a different model.
- Call a service, and use its answer.
- Remove a field before the data goes forward.
- Validate your output against a JSON schema.
