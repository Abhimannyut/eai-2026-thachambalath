## By the end of this session, you can

1. **Identify** the parts of a messaging flow: the producer, the consumer, the broker and the channel.
2. **Explain** the two parts of a message: the header and the body. Tell what belongs in each part.
3. **Explain** the difference between synchronous and asynchronous communication.
4. **Select** a channel type: point-to-point or publish-subscribe. Give a reason for your selection.
5. **Explain** store-and-forward. Tell why it removes temporal coupling.
6. **Recognise** the three delivery guarantees: at-most-once, at-least-once and exactly-once.
7. **Apply** a durable queue, publisher confirms and manual acknowledgement.
8. **Analyse** a messaging flow. Find the point where the flow loses a message.

## The hard parts

These are the parts that people find difficult. Read them before the
session. If you cannot follow one of them, make it your question.

- **Exactly-once delivery is not possible between two systems.** You get at-least-once delivery. Then you make the consumer safe against a repeated message. Session 5 gives the method.
- **The word durable applies to three different things:** the queue, the exchange and the message. You must set all three. If you set two of the three, you lose messages after a broker restart.
- **An acknowledgement is not a receipt.** It tells the broker that your work is complete. If you acknowledge the message first and then do the work, a crash loses the message.

## Self-check

Answer these before the session. They are not graded, and I do not
collect them. They show you which of the hard parts you cannot yet
explain. **A question that you cannot answer is your question for the
session.**

1. You publish a message. The broker restarts before a consumer reads the message. Is the message still in the queue?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Only if the queue is durable, the exchange is durable and the message is persistent. All three are necessary. Two of the three lose the message.

   </details>

2. You stop the consumer. The producer publishes 10 000 messages. What happens to them?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   They stay in the queue. The queue holds them until a consumer reads them, or until the messages expire. This is store-and-forward, and it is why messaging removes temporal coupling.

   </details>

3. A consumer receives the same message two times. Give two possible causes.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   The consumer did not acknowledge the message before a timeout, so the broker delivered it again. Or the producer sent the message two times after a failed publisher confirm. At-least-once delivery makes both normal.

   </details>

## What the assignment will require

**PA2 — RabbitMQ publish and consume**

You build a producer and a consumer. You then demonstrate three failure conditions.
The assignment assumes that you can do these things:
- Declare a durable queue.
- Use publisher confirms.
