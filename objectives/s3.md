8. **Read** and extend a service in Python.

## The hard parts

These are the parts that people find difficult. Read them before the
session. If you cannot follow one of them, make it your question.

- **An aggregator holds state.** The state is the difficult part, and not the routing. You must keep the results of each order separate until the order is complete.
- **A completion condition needs a timeout.** If one worker does not answer, an aggregator without a timeout waits forever. The correct behaviour is a partial result with a flag.
- **Two orders at the same time must not mix.** The correlation identifier keeps them apart. A duplicate message must not count two times.

## Self-check

Answer these before the session. They are not graded, and I do not
collect them. They show you which of the hard parts you cannot yet
explain. **A question that you cannot answer is your question for the
session.**

1. An order has three line items. The splitter sends three messages. Two workers answer, and one worker does not answer. What must the aggregator do?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   It waits until the timeout. Then it sends one completion message with two results, and it marks the result as partial. It does not wait forever, and it does not discard the two results.

   </details>

2. Two orders enter the flow at the same time. Each order has two line items. How does the aggregator keep the four results in the correct groups?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   It groups the results by the correlation identifier of the order. Each message carries the identifier of its order. Without the identifier the aggregator mixes the two orders.

   </details>

3. What is the difference between a recipient list and a publish-subscribe channel?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   A recipient list decides the receivers in the router. The sender controls the list. A publish-subscribe channel lets each consumer subscribe. The sender does not know the consumers.

   </details>
